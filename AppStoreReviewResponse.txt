App Review Response

Guideline 2.1 - Face Data

Heartelfie only processes face data during the optional facial rPPG wellness check. The app uses the front camera video stream to locate a face region and process subtle color changes that can be used to estimate pulse for a wellness screening. Heartelfie does not create Face ID templates, identify the user, or use face data for authentication, advertising, profiling, or tracking.

Raw face images and video frames are processed in memory on the device. They are not saved to the app database, not written to Apple Health, not uploaded to Heartelfie servers, and not shared with third parties. Raw face frames are discarded immediately after the check. Saved readings may include derived wellness metrics, signal quality values, timestamps, capture modality, model provenance, and a small derived waveform preview.

Face data is not sold, used for advertising, or shared with third parties. Raw face frames are not persistently stored. Derived readings are stored locally on the user's device in an encrypted database until the user deletes them, exports them, or removes the app. Users can delete local Heartelfie data from the app Data and Privacy screen.

The privacy policy explains face data in Section 1, Summary, Section 3, Face Data, Section 4, Storage, Retention, and Deletion, Section 6, Sharing and Third Parties, and Section 10, Security.

Relevant privacy policy text:

Section 1 states: "Camera frames, including face frames, are processed on device and are not saved." It also states: "Face data is not sold, used for advertising, or shared with third parties."

Section 3 states: "During a facial rPPG check, Heartelfie uses the front camera video stream to locate a face region and process subtle color changes that can be used to estimate pulse for a wellness screening. This is the only face data collected by the app. Heartelfie does not create Face ID templates, does not identify you, and does not use face data for authentication, advertising, profiling, or tracking."

Section 3 also states: "Raw face images and video frames are processed in memory on the device. They are not saved to the app database, not written to Apple Health, not uploaded to Heartelfie servers, and not shared with third parties. After the check ends, raw face frames are discarded."

Guideline 5.1.1 - HealthKit Permission Prompt

The HealthKit priming button has been changed from "Connect Apple Health" to "Continue" in onboarding and profile authorization flows. The app now clearly identifies the integration as "Apple Health and HealthKit" and explains that access is optional and controlled by the Health app.

Guideline 2.5.1 - HealthKit and CareKit Identification

The app does not use CareKit. The app uses HealthKit only through the optional Apple Health integration. The interface identifies this in onboarding, Profile, and Data and Privacy.

Guideline 1.4.1 - Safety and Medical Claims

In response to the July 23 review, we have removed the two device-sensor heart-measurement features the review identified. The app no longer rests the phone on the chest to sense heartbeat vibrations from the accelerometer and motion sensors (seismocardiography), and no longer holds the microphone to the chest to listen to heart sounds (phonocardiography). Both acquisition modalities, their sensor code, their associated metrics, their onboarding permission priming, and the NSMotionUsageDescription and NSMicrophoneUsageDescription entries have been removed from the app. The build no longer requests motion or microphone access and makes no health-measurement claim from those sensors.

Phone-based screening is now limited to camera-based photoplethysmography only: fingertip PPG using the rear camera and torch, and facial rPPG using the front camera. Any higher-confidence readings come only from the optional external Heartelfie hardware device.

The app has also been revised to reinforce wellness only positioning and remove reading driven medical action calls to action. Threshold notices now tell users to retake readings and compare trends, not to seek care based on app readings. Former hospital, emergency, and partner clinic action language has been removed from result cards.

Heartelfie is not represented as a medical device and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease or condition. If Heartelfie hardware is marketed as regulated medical hardware in any jurisdiction, we will attach the applicable regulatory clearance and validation documentation in App Store Connect before submitting in that jurisdiction.
